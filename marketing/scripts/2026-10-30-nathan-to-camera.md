## How to record

- Scene: **Ctrl+Alt+7** (his real room, softly blurred) or **Ctrl+Alt+1** (cut out over a Sabre home). Standing,
  or on a stool. Look at the lens, not the screen.
- One block at a time. Pause a breath between lines, so they cut cleanly.
- Each hook on its own, three times. Hooks are what get tested in ads, so we want lots of them.
- Two takes of each body. If he wants to say it his own way, let him: his words beat these.
- Ctrl+Alt+R starts and stops. Files land in `C:/Recordings`.

## 1. Introduction (about 45 seconds)

*Use: the site's about/home video, the start of longer videos, a cold-audience ad.*

> Hi, I'm Nathan from Sabre Constructions.
>
> We're a family building company. Mum and Dad started Sabre back in 1990, and we've been building on the
> Bayside ever since.
>
> A lot of the families we talk to are in the same spot.
>
> They love where they live. The street, the school, the neighbours.
>
> But the house just doesn't fit them anymore.
>
> So instead of selling up and moving away, we knock the old house down and build a new one, right there on
> the same block.
>
> The way it works is pretty simple.
>
> First, I come out and walk through your house with you. What's working, what's not, and what your family
> needs.
>
> Then we help you with the designers, so the new house is drawn around your family and your block.
>
> Then we build it. And we're with you every step of the way.
>
> If that sounds like you, book a walk-through. I'll give you a call and we'll find a time that suits.

## 2. Angle A: Stay on the street you love (about 30 seconds)

*The idea: don't move out of the area to get more room. Upgrade the house, keep the life.*

**Hooks (record each one on its own):**
1. "Love your street, but the house is too small?"
2. "You don't have to leave the area to get a bigger house."
3. "Same street. Same school. A whole new house."

**Body:**
> Most families we meet aren't looking to move.
>
> The kids are at the local school. Their mates are just down the road.
>
> The problem's the house. Not enough bedrooms, one bathroom for everyone, a kitchen nobody fits in.
>
> A knockdown rebuild means you keep all the good stuff. The street, the school, the neighbours.
>
> You just end up with a house that actually fits your family.
>
> Book a walk-through and I'll come and have a look.

---

## 3. Angle B: Custom means built around you (about 30 seconds)

*The idea: "custom" doesn't mean fancy. It means the house is worked out around the family.*

**Hooks:**
1. "Custom home doesn't mean a fancy home."
2. "When we say custom, we mean built around your family."
3. "Why squeeze your family into a plan made for someone else?"

**Body:**
> When people hear "custom builder", they think big and fancy.
>
> That's not what it means to us.
>
> It means the house is worked out around your family. How many kids. Where they sleep. Where dinner happens.
> Where all the stuff goes.
>
> Not a plan off a shelf you have to fit your family into.
>
> It starts with me walking through your house with you, room by room, and you telling me what's stopped
> working.
>
> Then the designers draw it around your family and your block.
>
> Book a walk-through and we'll start there.

*Only if Nathan confirms it's true, an extra line:* "And custom doesn't have to mean it costs more." Then it needs
his reason (e.g. how they keep the cost down). Lesley W's real review backs this up and can go on screen:
"The first thing Stewart pointed out was the plans could be changed to build it cheaper."

---

## 4. Angle C: One builder, the whole way (about 30 seconds)

*The idea: a family builder who stays with you, not a company with a sales team and a hand-off.*

**Hooks:**
1. "The builder who walks your house should be the one who hands you the keys."
2. "We only take on a handful of homes at a time. Here's why."
3. "We're a family, building family homes."

**Body:**
> We're a family business. Mum and Dad started Sabre in 1990.
>
> We only take on a handful of homes at a time, and that's on purpose.
>
> It means the person who walks through your old house with you is the same person answering the phone the
> whole way through. Right up until we hand you the keys.
>
> No sales team. No hand-off.
>
> Thirty-six years on the Bayside, and that's what people say about us. We stay with you.
>
> Book a walk-through and you'll see what I mean.
