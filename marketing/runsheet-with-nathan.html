The plan in three stages

Prove the system works with an everyday campaign, then push the 2027 offer over the holidays, then go back to everyday ads aimed at whoever actually turned into a customer.

Stage 1 · one week · from ~9 Nov

General campaign

Simple everyday ads on Google and Facebook/Instagram. "A family builder on the Bayside since 1990."

  • Leads land in Nathan's inbox and the CRM
  • We see which suburbs and searches bring enquiries
  • We learn the real cost per enquiry
Stage 2 · from ~16 Nov → sign-by date

End-of-year push

"Get in now for a 2027 build." A dated offer with limited places, while families talk about the house over the holidays.

  • Same ads system, new offer ads on top
  • Past clients and referrers hear about it first
  • Ends on Nathan's sign-by date
Stage 3 · once it's all working

General again, sharper

Back to everyday ads, aimed at the people stages 1 and 2 showed us are real buyers.

  • Money moves to the suburbs and searches that worked
  • Kill the worst ad, back the best, one new test a month
⚠️ "Go-live" needs the real domain. Ads can't send people to the github.io preview, so stage 1 starts the day the new site is on sabreconstructions.com.au. Google Ads also needs the business check to pass, which can take up to 5 working days.

General campaign · Facebook and Instagram

Meta's job: put "knock down and rebuild" in front of families who haven't searched yet but own an older house and are outgrowing it.

Who sees it

  • People living in the same suburbs as Google
  • Ages 30–60 (young families plus the parents who help them)
  • Later: people who watched a Sabre video or visited the site

Split

  • 60% new people in the suburbs
  • 25% people who've watched or liked Sabre
  • 15% site visitors who didn't enquire

The last two switch on once there's traffic to follow up.

Where they land

  • The home page, or the suburb page
  • The goal is the same as Google: book a walk-through with Nathan

End-of-year push · "Get in now for 2027"

The same Google and Meta setup keeps running. On top of it go offer ads with a real deadline and a set number of places, and past clients and referrers hear about it first.

"Your 2027 family rebuild: demolition included, plus the [name] upgrade pack worth $[X]. Only [N] families, sign by [date]."

Why the end of the year

Families talk about the house over the summer holidays. A 2027 start needs design and council approval first, so they have to begin talking now.

What changes in the ads

  • Google: an offer line added to the ads
  • Meta: new offer ads, with a countdown to the sign-by date
  • The home page gets an offer banner

Warm first

  • Past clients
  • Neighbours of current sites
  • Architects and brokers who send work

After that · general again, aimed at real buyers

By then we'll know from real enquiries, not guesses:

Where

Which suburbs brought enquiries. The money follows them.

What

Which searches and which ads turned into walk-throughs.

Who

What the real buyers look like: young family, parents helping, or downsizers.

Cost

What one enquiry costs, compared with what one build is worth.

Rules for reading it: wait at least 7 days or $1,000 before judging anything. Weekly, write the numbers down. Monthly, kill the worst, back the best, and start one new test.

Budget

Sabre pays Google and Meta directly on its own card. Our proposal is roughly $30–50 a day across both, until Nathan picks a number.
