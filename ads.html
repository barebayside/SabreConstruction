Concept 01 · Ready to run

The Rebuild

"This was a 1970s lowset.
Same block. Same street."

The strongest asset you have. A tired brick-and-tile comes down and a new home assembles itself in its place, landing on a real Sabre build. It stops the scroll because nobody expects a house to go up in ten seconds.

  • 0–2sOld lowset sits there. Caption: "This was a 1970s lowset."
  • 2–5sIt comes down. Dust. No music cut — let it land.
  • 5–13sNew home assembles piece by piece. This is the retention driver.
  • 13–16sFinished home holds. Caption: "Knockdown rebuilds, Brisbane Bayside." CTA card.
How it's made Already built. The 16:9 master is on the landing page; this is the 9:16 cut. Generated as two segments (demolition, then assembly) with fixed start and end frames, so the final frame is Sabre's real photograph rather than an invented house.

Concept 02 · Needs filming

The Walk-through

"Everyone asks me the same
question on day one."

Stewart or Nathan on a real site, phone-filmed, talking straight to camera for eight seconds — then the frame transforms into the finished home. The authenticity of the first half is what makes the second half land.

  • 0–3sFace to camera, hard hat, real frame behind him. The hook line.
  • 3–8sHe answers it plainly. One idea, no list.
  • 8–14sFrame transforms around him into the finished home.
  • 14–16s"Book a free site visit." Phone number on screen.
How it's made Send the raw clip. I pull the last frame with ffmpeg, feed it in as the start image, and generate the transformation forward from it — so the join is invisible because it's literally the same pixels. Shoot it vertical: the AI half inherits the aspect from that last frame.

Concept 03 · Needs real customers

The Straight Answer

"I never had to chase anyone."

A past client, filmed plainly, saying one specific thing. Not a highlight reel — one sentence about one worry they had. This is the retargeting ad: it runs at people who already visited the page and didn't book.

  • 0–3sStraight into the sentence. No intro, no logo sting.
  • 3–8sThey expand on it in their own words.
  • 8–12sCut to the finished house. Name and suburb on screen.
How it's made The clip shown here is an AI stand-in so you can see the format — it is not a real customer, which is why it carries a burned-in label. To actually run this, film two or three real clients on a phone. Fifteen minutes each.

Filming the walk-through

Phone is fine — better than fine. Hold it vertical, get the sun behind you, stand close enough that the mic picks up your voice over the site noise, and say one thing. Don't script it word for word; know the sentence you're opening with and let the rest come out how it comes out. Twenty seconds of usable footage is all I need.
