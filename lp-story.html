Should you move,
or rebuild?

The honest version — including the parts most builders don't bring up until after you've signed.

You've outgrown the house. You haven't outgrown the street.

That's the whole problem, and it's why the decision is so much harder than it looks from the outside. Moving means a new school run, new neighbours, and a mortgage set by whatever the market is doing that month. Staying means living through a build.

Most families we meet have been going round on this for a year or more before they ring anyone.

It's not the house. It's the year.

Nobody lies awake over cornice profiles. Here's what they actually lie awake over, in the order it usually comes up:

Worry one Where do the kids live? Rent nearby and carry two payments, or move out of the catchment and change schools mid-year. Almost nobody raises this with you before you've committed. It's usually the first thing you thought of.
Worry two A price that won't sit still. You budgeted around a family, not a project. A variation isn't a line item — it's the holiday, the second car, or the buffer you were counting on if something went wrong.
Worry three Nobody picking up. You're running drop-offs, work and a build at once. Three weeks of silence isn't an inconvenience. It's a thing you can't plan around, and you have to plan around everything.

What we do about each one.

Where you'll live goes on the table at the site visit, before anything is signed. It's the biggest cost people forget, and it changes the timing conversation completely. If renting nearby for nine months breaks the numbers, better to know now.

The price is itemised and fixed before you commit. Change your mind later — and most families do on something — and we price the change and show you what it does to the total before we touch it.

The phone is Frank, who's run our sites for seventeen years, and Celene in the office. Same two people, slab to handover. You'll know what's happening on site this week without chasing it.
