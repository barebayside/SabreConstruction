## His words

**Custom doesn't mean fancy** (3:23) → positioning, R5, Angle B
> "Custom doesn't mean it's architecturally built, with all these crazy curves and different designs. It's
> more having the layout they'd like in their home, one that suits them. Whether that's a Hamptons style or
> anything like that, mainly it's having the rooms where they want them. That's the main thing for us as
> custom builders, not architectural builders. A lot of people get those two confused."

**Do I need an architect?** (4:12) → R5, knockdown-rebuild page
> "At some point, yes, you'll need an architect to design what you'd like built. The block size and the
> shape of the block will change dramatically what you can fit on there and what kind of layout. And if
> the street's heritage listed, you may need to tie in with the other buildings around you."
>
> "But the starting point is working out whether you want to knock your house down and start again, or
> raise the house up and build in underneath. It just depends on what you want."

**Who draws it, and the engineer** (5:04) → R5
> "We work with a couple of architects ourselves. Some of our clients deal with their own architects,
> which is fine. We work pretty well with different architects."
>
> ⚠️ "The one we like to keep in-house is the structural side, all the engineering. We want to use our
> engineer, the one we've been using for 20 years. It makes the building process a lot smoother."

**Picking taps and tiles** (6:06) → S15 variations, R45 what's in a quote
> "Everyone's got a different opinion on what looks good. So for tapware and that, we usually allow a PS
> item, a set amount for those fixtures. Most of the time that changes by the time you get close to
> putting them in. Most people have changed their mind by then."
>
> "We give you enough notice: we're getting close, are these definitely the tiles you want, the fixtures
> you picked before? That gives you the chance to change, and whether that costs more or less, we sort it."
>
> ⚠️ He said "90% of the time". Not a counted figure, so the line above uses "most of the time".

**How long a build takes** (7:25) → S5
> ⚠️ "Every house is different: how big it is, how much is involved, the access. But usually from start to
> finish you'd be looking at somewhere between six and twelve months."

**The family company** (8:02) → meet-the-team, R16
> ⚠️ "We're a family-run building company. Stewart started the company back in the 90s, and he's now
> wanting to step back and slow down. He'll stay on the quoting, and I'm taking over pretty much all the
> day to day."

**Two builders** (9:05) → positioning, R16
> "We've got two people who've been in the building game a while, myself and Stewart. Sometimes on a job
> you need that old way of thinking, or the new way of thinking. It helps us solve problems a lot easier
> than one person trying to figure out all the answers."

**Why he prefers knockdown rebuilds** (10:04) → S2, knockdown-rebuild page, R14
> "We generally like to do knockdown rebuilds. It gives you a fresh slate, a brand new home. It's easier
> to get the outcome you're really after."
>
> "We do a lot of renovations too, house raising, extensions. But you're trying to tie the old into the
> new, and nothing's ever perfect. That's probably the biggest concern for a lot of people: they want it
> all brand new, and technically it's not. You make it as seamless as you can."

**Where they build** (10:53) → `/wynnum/`, `/manly/`, R16
> ⚠️ "We've built pretty much all through Wynnum, Manly, Lota. We work about a 30-minute radius. We used
> to go a lot further, Brighton, Ipswich, the Redlands. Now we're bringing it back in, to work with local
> people and build the communities around us."

**Being local** (12:41) → `/wynnum/`, S7
> "I've grown up in this area my whole life. I went to school in Wynnum, all my friends were there. I know
> the area off by heart, the streets, the back streets, the local people. So I like to stay here and help
> build the community up, with the housing families want."
