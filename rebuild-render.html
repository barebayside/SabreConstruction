Knockdown rebuild · One locked camera

Their street. Their new home.

One photograph taken from the footpath, one set of the architect's drawings, and a video that takes the old house down and puts the new one up without the camera ever moving. Nothing here was filmed and nothing here is a stock house.

⚠ Concept visualisation. The cleared block and the finished home are generated images, built from the owner's photo and their architect's elevation. They are an artist's impression of an approved design, not a photograph of a completed build.

The film

Sixteen seconds, start to finish.

The old house comes down, the slab goes in, the frames stand up, the trusses and roof follow, and the finished home lands on the last frame. Because the camera never moves, the eye stays locked on the one thing that changes — which is the whole point of a rebuild ad.

16 seconds · 16:9 · silent · loops. A 9:16 cut for Reels and Stories comes off the same master.

How it's built

Three frames, then the motion.

Every frame is derived from the owner's own photograph, so the footpath, the power pole, the neighbours and the frangipani tree sit in exactly the same place in all three. That's what stops the finished house from looking like it was pasted onto a different street.

⚠ The design isn't ours and neither are the drawings. The finished home is another practice's design. Written permission from the designer, and from the owners, is needed before this runs anywhere public — and the address and the owners' names stay off it either way.

Where it runs

What this is actually for.

Selling the idea, not the house

Most people can't picture their own street without their own house on it. This shows them, using their street. It's the single hardest objection in a knockdown rebuild and it's answered in ten seconds.

One per job, at almost no cost

Every rebuild Sabre quotes already has the two ingredients: a site photo and a set of drawings. Turning them into a clip like this costs a couple of dollars and about an hour.

Feeds three places at once

The 16:9 master for the website and Google, a 9:16 cut for Reels and Stories, and the three stills for a carousel or a proposal PDF.
